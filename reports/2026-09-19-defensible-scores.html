1. Executive brief

  • What Nathan can defend: Score-only LLM judgments are a weak protocol. Requiring analysis or explanation with the rating improves correlation with human ratings on NLG meta-eval; structured evidence-then-score is a different (and safer) intervention than unconstrained free-form CoT. Consistency is not validity. verified
  • Best single footnote: Chiang & Lee, Findings of EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2310.05657 — numeric-only “suboptimal”; analyze-rate / rate-explain beat or match score-only; auto-CoT scaffolding does not reliably help. verified
  • Grok author misnames: “Öncel et al.” → Shakur et al. (2410.12858); “Holden et al.” → Geathers et al. (2501.13957). author misnamed
  • Consistency ≠ validity: Tiwari 2026 — local judges 97.3% / 92.3% exact self-consistency vs Pearson r 0.275 / 0.340 with humans. Geathers GPT-4o Krippendorff’s α = 0.98 at T=0 with low exact accuracy (0.27–0.44). verified
  • Recommended protocol: observations (with transcript spans) → map to published rubric anchors → derive score as a function of the map → human audit of the mapping (not self-consistency alone). verified as synthesis of Shakur + QAA + classic oral standards — not a named MeSH finding.
Talking sentence: Chiang and Lee (2023) show that score-only LLM ratings underperform the same judge when it must rationalize the rating; the number alone is a weaker evaluation protocol. The gain is from constraining the rating with evidence — not from longer context as such.

3. The claim, tightened

Defensible restatement (from Grok, largely supportable):

Score-only LLM judgments are a weak protocol. Chiang and Lee (2023) find that requiring a rationale with the rating improves human correlation and that numeric-only output is suboptimal; G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023) independently shows discrete score tokens can concentrate on one digit (e.g. 3 on a 1–5 scale). Evaluator models such as Prometheus are trained to emit rubric-grounded feedback before a score (Kim et al., 2024). The gain is from constraining the rating with evidence, not from longer context as such. Unstructured free-form reasoning can add variance rather than remove it.

Chiang & Lee guideline (verbatim direction)

“Always ask ChatGPT to explain/analyze when rating.” “Using auto CoT is optional since it does not always lead to higher correlations.” — Chiang & Lee 2023 · verified
“forcing the LLM to output only a numeric rating, as in G-Eval, is suboptimal.” — Chiang & Lee 2023 · verified

Nuance Nathan should keep: Chiang finds rate-explain (score before rationale) ≈ analyze-rate; neither significantly better than the other. So “rationale-then-score uniquely wins over score-then-rationale” is Grok overstated relative to Chiang. Permission / requirement to explain still beats bare score-only.

4.2 Liu et al. G-Eval — partial (midpoint / digit dominance)

G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment. EMNLP 2023 main, pp. 2511–2522. arXiv:2303.16634 · ACL · DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.153 verified PDF · partial as “midpoint collapse law”

  • G-Eval-4 average Spearman ρ = 0.514 on SummEval (Table 1); with CoT beats −CoT (AVG ρ 0.514 vs 0.500).
  • Quote: “For some evaluation tasks, one digit usually dominates the distribution of the scores, such as 3 for a 1 - 5 scale.” Motivates probability-weighted score \(\sum_i p(s_i)\, s_i\).
  • Also: LLMs usually output integer scores even when decimals requested → many ties.
  • Grok “collapse toward the scale midpoint” as a general law: Grok overstated — paper reports dominance of one digit (illustrated by 3), not a quantified multi-scale midpoint experiment.
  • Note conflict: Chiang finds auto-CoT not always helpful for ChatGPT; G-Eval’s own CoT ablation helps GPT-4 on SummEval.
  • Flagged low human IAA in one setting: Krippendorff’s α = 0.07.

4.4 Kim et al. Prometheus — training format

Prometheus: Inducing Fine-grained Evaluation Capability in Language Models. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.08491 · project page verified

  • Feedback Collection: 1K rubrics, 20K instructions, 100K GPT-4 (response, feedback, score) tuples.
  • Training target: feedback (rationale) + integer score 1–5 given instruction, response, rubric, reference.
  • Pearson 0.897 with humans on 45 customized rubrics (GPT-4 0.882; ChatGPT 0.392).
  • Accurate that serious evaluators are trained for rubric-grounded feedback-then-score; not a score-only vs rationale ablation of a frozen judge. partial as causal proof of format.

5. Clinical / OSCE transfer

Bottom line: extract→rationale→score is real (Shakur). High self-consistency at T=0 is real (Geathers). Neither proves rationale-first always improves human validity over bare scores on multi-item ordinal rubrics.

7. CoT vs “larger context” — background only

NOT the main claim. Track (A) is score-format / evidence-then-rating. This section is track (B) mechanism background so the two stay separate. CoT-origin lore can be skipped when talking about defensible scores.

Slogan under check: “CoT worked because next-word prediction is more accurate with larger context.” Wrong as a causal story.

Passive context length

How much the model reads. Attention over a longer prefix. Can hurt even with perfect retrieval (Du et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025: ~13.9%–85% relative degradation). arXiv:2510.05381 · Anthology

Generated CoT length

How much serial work the model is allowed to write. Extra forward passes + scratchpad. Expressivity scales with intermediate steps t(n) (Merrill & Sabharwal; Li et al.).

  • Wei et al. 2022 arXiv:2201.11903: variable-compute dots ≈ baseline (~6.4% vs ~6.5% standard on GSM8K approx.); thought-after-answer ≈ baseline; full CoT ~14.3%. Contentful pre-answer steps matter. verified from mechanism note.
  • Pfau et al. 2024 arXiv:2404.15758: fillers can help as trained compute slots on some parallelizable tasks — still not “passive context accuracy.”
  • Merrill & Sabharwal arXiv:2310.07923; Li et al. arXiv:2402.12875: serial depth / scratchpad expressivity.
  • Quanta (Brubaker, 21 Mar 2024): How CoT Helps Neural Networks Compute — accessible serial-compute framing.

Oral-scoring analogy (clarity only): a bare score token after a long transcript is a high-entropy global prediction; structured evidence→rubric→score is closer to decomposition of the judge’s own output, not stuffing more transcript into context.

8. Recommended protocol for oral-exam-like scoring

Research / pilot design — not a certification standard. Matches Nathan’s “defensible” framing while staying faithful to what papers support.

Inputs

  • High-quality audio → ASR with QC (Shakur ~2% discard lesson).
  • Published analytic rubric with observable anchors (behaviorally worded 1–5 or binary).
  • Blueprint mapping oral competencies → items (content validity separate from LLM agreement).

Model output contract (per rubric item)

  1. observations[] — atomic claims, each with transcript_span (quote + timestamp if available). Inventory of positive and negative evidence — not only the first deficiency (anchoring risk).
  2. anchor_map — for each observation, which published anchor level it supports (or “insufficient evidence”).
  3. derived_score — deterministic rule or constrained choice from the filled map (score is a function of the map, not a free token).
  4. confidence / flags — missing audio, conflicting spans, out-of-rubric content, possible ASR error.
  5. Optional separate feedback_text for learners — do not let feedback narrative rewrite the score.

Decoding & aggregation

  • Primary: T=0 (or low T); log model version + prompt hash.
  • Optional: small sample at T>0 + mean/median for uncertainty (GEM-style), especially on soft items.
  • Do not show prior human or prior machine scores (anchoring literature: 2608.25869).

Human audit (the “defensible” part)

  • Sample all fails / borderlines + random passes.
  • Audit mapping fidelity: do quoted spans exist? Does the anchor mapping follow the rubric text?
  • Track LLM–human κ / ICC / Krippendorff α per domain, not only global.
  • Monitor year/case/station drift (Shakur year effect).
  • Multimodal claim-check where physical/procedural claims matter (Rogers et al. QAA direction: arXiv:2607.19063 — error detection P 69.9% / R 76.7%; correcting errors raises factually correct transcripts 39.2% → 79.2%).

A/B arms for an ABPMR-adjacent pilot

ArmHypothesis
Score-onlyHigher entropy / worse human κ
Free CoT then scoreMay stabilize wrong features; Geathers: not uniformly better
Structured observations→anchors→scoreBetter auditability; test whether human κ rises
Ensemble + escalate on disagreementHigher precision, lower coverage (Shakur ensemble pattern)

Success criteria: chance-corrected agreement with calibrated examiners; acceptable bias (Bland–Altman); stable under prompt paraphrase; documented adverse-impact check — not self-consistency alone.

9. What is still unknown / overclaim risks

  • Does adding a rationale improve validity, or merely reduce sampling variance and lock in a biased first impression?
  • Are cited observations faithful causes of the score, or post-hoc justifications? (Turpin et al. unfaithful CoT: arXiv:2305.04388; Somov et al. Breaking the Chain.)
  • Causal contribution of serial forward passes vs meaningful decomposition vs distribution matching — still mechanism debate, not settled for judges.
  • When does unconstrained CoT hurt reliability vs when structured protocol helps? OpenReview numbers fetch limited.
  • Transfer from NLG meta-eval (G-Eval, MT-Bench, Chiang) to ABPMR-style oral/clinical exams is hypothesis, not proven.
  • No published LLM κ on scoring human physiatry / ABPMR oral candidates against many-facet Rasch or calibrated examiner panels.
  • Yamauchi et al. counterweight: with clear criteria + reference, CoT ≈ direct scoring (arXiv:2506.13639 · GEM 2026) — does not refute Chiang on weak/no-rubric NLG settings.
  • Hong et al. Rulers (arXiv:2601.08654) and Tapwal et al. Proof-Before-Preference (arXiv:2605.23970) are closest “evidence-locking” engineering cousins — verify full-PDF numbers before hard citation.
Overclaim risks: Do not call this AGI. Do not invent psychometrics. Do not equate self-consistency with validity. Do not treat κ=0.88 or α=0.98 as board-ready. Do not cite “defensible scores” as if it were a published MeSH finding.
